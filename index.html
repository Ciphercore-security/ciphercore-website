// Resources

Guides and tips you can use today

Free, practical material — no booking required.

Password hygiene

Use a unique password per account, a password manager to store them, and MFA everywhere it's offered — especially email and banking.

Spotting a phishing email

Check the sender's actual address, not just the display name. Be wary of urgency and links that don't match the claimed sender.

Home & office Wi-Fi basics

Change default router passwords, use WPA3 or WPA2 encryption, and keep a separate network for guests and IoT devices.

If you think you've been breached

Disconnect the affected device, change passwords from a separate device, and contact your IT or security provider immediately.

Backups that actually work

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on two different storage types, with one kept offsite or offline.

Social engineering red flags

Be cautious of unexpected urgency, requests to bypass normal process, and callers asking to "verify" information.
