// about ciphercore

Built by young professionals. Driven by technology. Focused on the gaps.

CipherCore Security is a youth-led technology company created by a group of young professionals who saw an opportunity to turn their technical knowledge into practical solutions for organisations.

We started with a simple question: where are organisations vulnerable, and how can we help close those gaps?

Organisations rely on technology every day, but weaknesses can exist in their systems, networks, processes and — importantly — in the way people interact with technology.

CipherCore aims to identify these gaps, understand the risks they create and help organisations develop practical ways to address them.

Our work combines cybersecurity awareness, technical exploration, IT support and practical security demonstrations to make digital security easier to understand.

We're not trying to present ourselves as a company that already knows everything.

// can you spot it

This is what we train your team to catch

A real phishing email rarely looks obviously fake. It looks like it's already inside your inbox, from someone you'd trust.

1
Sender domain is close, not exact. "ciphercore-secure-update.com" is not our real domain — easy to miss at a glance.
2
Manufactured urgency. "Immediately" and a countdown push you to act before you think.
3
Generic greeting. A real colleague or supplier usually uses your name.
4
Link text hides the real destination. What's shown and where it actually goes are rarely the same.
From: IT Support <alerts@ciphercore-secure-update.com> 1
Subject: Immediately verify your account — action required 2
To: you@yourcompany.co.za

Hi there, 3

We detected unusual sign-in activity on your account. To avoid suspension, please confirm your details within 24 hours.

Verify my account now 4

// resources

Guides, tips, and awareness cards

Free, practical material you can put to use today — no consultation required.

guide

Password hygiene checklist

Use a unique password per account, a password manager to store them, and MFA everywhere it's offered — especially email and banking.

tip card

Spotting a phishing email

Check the sender's actual address, not just the display name. Be wary of urgency, unexpected attachments, and links that don't match the claimed sender.

guide

Home & office Wi-Fi basics

Change default router passwords, use WPA3 or WPA2 encryption, and keep a separate network for guests and IoT devices.

tip card

If you think you've been breached

Disconnect the affected device from the network, change passwords from a separate device, and contact your IT or security provider immediately — don't wait to "make sure."

guide

Backups that actually work

Follow the 3-2-1 rule: three copies of your data, on two different types of storage, with one kept offsite or offline.

tip card

Social engineering red flags

Be cautious of unexpected urgency, requests to bypass normal process, and callers or emails asking to "verify" information they should already have.
